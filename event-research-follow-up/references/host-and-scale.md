# Host conversations and conditional expansion

These are proposed management checkpoints, not validated conversion rates or permanent instructions to expand. Adapt batch size to available delivery capacity and user scope.

## Four useful angles

| Route | Discovery question | Useful next step |
|---|---|---|
| Direct services | Which business unit, customer segment and growth market needs help, and who owns it? | One measured search/AI-visibility brief or defined revenue-workflow outline, then a working session with the owner if requested. |
| Delivery partner | Which client task falls outside your own strengths or capacity? | One real package with agreed scope, client ownership, quality, price and margin. |
| Operating-company introduction | Is there one suitable company where this specific problem matters? | Offer a resource, then request an appropriate introduction with permission. Parent/fund size does not qualify that company. |
| Educational collaboration | Would a practical example or workshop help your clients or community? | One joint session or example after interest and attribution/material permissions are confirmed. |

## Host sequence

Before the event, roughly one to two weeks ahead, prepare:

> Looking forward to seeing you at the summit. As I prepare the session, what is one growth or revenue problem you would most like AI to help with? A sentence or two would help me make the discussion useful.

At the event, ask their market and problem. Show verified or clearly labeled demonstration work. Ask:

> Would it be useful if I sent you a short brief for that specific problem afterward?

Record the business/unit, problem, market, recipient, permission, promised resource and delivery owner. Limit promises to preparation capacity. An optional resource link is a separate approved asset; it must not imply an automatic sales sequence.

Deliver a requested resource within two to three business days. Post-event copy must use the real conversation and an asset that exists. Invite the owner only after interest. No generic meeting request or automatic follow-up sequence is created by this plan.

For an event the user does not host, use only a truthful shared context. Test it separately: host response must not be assumed to transfer.

## Initial, double and triple tests

Initial cohort: ten distinct company groups, with one primary contact each. Coordinate co-leaders. Suggested target: four to five requested follow-ups; this is a target, not a forecast.

Double: at least four groups request a resource; deliver those resources; obtain at least two qualified opportunities including one scoped proposal or priced discussion; confirm capacity. Evaluate two comparable event cohorts and prepare up to twenty additional company groups. This is a replication test, not proof of a working channel.

Triple: at least one paid engagement with positive contribution after acquisition/delivery costs, a qualified opportunity from another event cohort, reliable follow-through and available capacity. Evaluate three comparable events and up to thirty additional company groups. Expand only supported routes.

Direct qualification requires supported operating-company fit, a stated need, owner and agreed next step. Partner qualification requires a specific client/project, complementary task, owner and commercial discussion path. A promised introduction alone is not a qualified opportunity.

If the test misses, identify the failed stage and change one factor: audience, question, offer or follow-through. More events are not the default remedy. Long sales cycles leave results unresolved; record that honestly.

## Measure and learn

Track companies selected, outreach attempts, actual conversations, requested resources, delivery, qualified opportunities, proposals, signed work, collected revenue and contribution. Count each company once in overall unique-company totals. Preserve its membership in each event cohort for cohort-level denominators, without double-counting the same opportunity or revenue. Separate new acquisition from repeat relationship progress and preserve event attribution.

Report clients, partners and introductions separately. Track qualified opportunities per owner-hour, realized contribution per acquisition hour, time to deliver, and unresolved cases. Include travel, preparation, team labor and API/enrichment costs. Contribution means collected revenue minus acquisition and delivery costs; show the user's hours separately unless a cost rate is agreed. Review 30/60/90-day outcomes with the full denominator.

Deepen a partner only after a successful project and workable economics. Multiple projects are a hypothesis until actual work proves it. Do not treat inferred access or friendliness as a repeatable channel.

Planning rhythm: first month prepare a small cohort and resources; second month run the event approach and requested follow-through; third month evaluate replication/depth. Event availability and evidence control timing. No recurring job is enabled.

Assign a strategy owner for follow-through and a delivery owner for feasibility. Before any external action review the recipients/channel/text and applicable approval. Before a new paid batch resolve source scope, record ceiling, contract, budget and stop condition.

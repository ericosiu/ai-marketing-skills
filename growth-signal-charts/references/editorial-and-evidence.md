# Editorial examples and evidence handling

Use this when researching an angle or writing the story. The rules support the Growth Signal identity; they do not turn one software example into a required topic.

## Research record

For each claim, preserve:

- Publisher, exact title, URL, publication date, and date checked.
- Fieldwork or observation dates, geography, sample, and population when disclosed.
- Metric definition, unit, exact values, and any calculation you performed.
- Study limitations and whether the finding is observed, self-reported, estimated, or causal.
- Whether another source independently measures the same claim, offers related context, or contradicts it.

A publication in the last 30 days can use older fieldwork. Keep those dates separate. Mark older context explicitly. A social post's recency does not refresh its underlying evidence.

Cross-reference the argument, not just the wording. Rankings and time on page are different outcomes; one cannot validate the other's exact effect. Enterprise EBIT and self-reported ROI are also different measures. Reposts of the same study are not independent corroboration. If only one primary source supports a number, cite it accurately and say what remains unverified rather than inventing a second validation.

Use genuine comparable data on a shared axis. Avoid turning responses to different survey questions into a conversion funnel, stacking overlapping answers, or attributing an observational difference to a cause the study did not establish.

## Candidate brief

A compact candidate table should show the original claim/source, source-match status, measured post signal, proposed chart finding, freshness, primary source, chart form, buyer problem or reach purpose, and reason to try or hold it. Use [selection and performance](selection-and-performance.md) for the matching and ranking rules. Keep the shortlist proportional to the user's request.

For Instagram, make the visual finding understandable quickly and the application worth saving. For X, write a direct claim and concise implication that stand with the attached chart; verify the caption fits the intended posting mode. For LinkedIn, explain the operating implication, a concrete tradeoff, and the key qualification. Preserve the same factual meaning across platforms. Use the requested current export specifications; do not imply that changing dimensions or captions is a proven performance improvement.

Do not reuse competitor artwork or claim that copying a layout will reproduce its performance. User-provided analytics can support observations about those posts; they cannot isolate design as the cause of reach.

## Keep examples and evidence separate

The public package contains a [style guide](desk-edition-style.md), not production chart values or a private concept board. If a layout needs sample data, label it clearly as fictional and keep it out of production claims. A source is attached only after verifying the actual measure.

For a software-buying question, a study about foregoing a purchase does not establish how much total software spending was replaced, savings achieved, or reliability of an internally built tool. Show the supported measure and put any practical cost comparison in the caption. Do not invent a second chart from a checklist.

## Keep the brand reusable

The series identity comes from the rail, open-loop mark, typography, whitespace and source position. No fixed statistic, software topic, slide count or decorative illustration is required.

New color or layout experiments should state what changed, how the chart helps the subject, and whether the style has been selected. Keep experiment images outside the skill's approved references until the user chooses them.

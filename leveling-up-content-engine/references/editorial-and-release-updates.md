# Editorial and release updates

Portable adaptation of the installed Leveling Up Content Engine workflow, reviewed September 21, 2026. Private episode history, internal paths, account identifiers, media and brand assets are intentionally excluded. Current explicit user directions override defaults.

## Editor instructions and persistent preferences

Before planning any recording edit, inspect the entire source for spoken editor directions, including before the take, retakes, demonstrations, ads and the ending. Read current written notes. Create `editor-instructions.md` with an ID, exact source timestamp, request, required/optional/superseded state, planned treatment and verification for each instruction. Preserve superseded directions; apply later clear corrections. Record coverage when none are found.

Remove production chatter from the audience cut while fulfilling its directions. Inventory all screen shares and separate ads, verify synchronization, and carry requested website footage into the shot plan. Use existing speech when new narration is declined. Report ad placement from the current export. Before delivery, reconcile every required instruction to the current render and disclose unresolved items; explain optional omissions.

Load a project-owned editing preference profile before each full-video run. Record its ID, version and hash. Carry the profile in every advisory review request; do not assume a model remembers it. Keep private profiles outside this public package. Maintain a change ledger with finding origin, before evidence, judgment, edit, final timestamp/version/hash and audiovisual verification.

## Rank moments before rendering

Inventory complete stories across the full source. Compare hook, stakes, specific evidence, turning point and useful payoff before selecting cuts. A title-support check on a preselected clip does not prove the best moment was found. Approval to release a clip is not evidence that it was the preferred moment.

For every candidate, record the exact spoken hook, story/tension, evidence with its meaning and limits, and final useful takeaway. Reject endings that only restate a category or promise unproven benefits. A complete sentence, polished diagram or technical pass is insufficient payoff. Record per-asset hook/payoff/ending review separately from source-boundary checks.

## Proof-led visual treatment

Open with the concrete viewer payoff. For demonstrations, show real results early, then explanation and examples. For comparisons, preview the evidence without prematurely spoiling the result. Plan the opening clause by clause using relevant proof, readable typography and purposeful picture changes. Do not impose motion merely to meet a timer.

For trend-led cutdowns, name the relevant trend or product and its source-supported application/payoff in both platform copy and the visible opening. Use authentic supplied marks and relevant moving proof in the first three to four seconds. Continue useful visuals through the explanation and supply a source-faithful cover. Metadata alone does not fix the opening. Never invent popularity or measured engagement lift.

Use face, readable proof, short graphics and face again where the source supports it. Reveal named logos at the matching spoken names when required by the brief. Use progressive lists, output close-ups and intentional screen crops. Check captions and claim qualifiers for collisions. Preserve vendor-demo, simulation, target and early-test labels. An agent's connected actions are not proof that a separate classification tool performs them.

## Advisory model review

Optional semantic checks cover promise fulfillment, complete thought, evidence, missing context and distinct angle. Keep IDs, versions, duration, timing and state transitions in code. Assess title and actual opening paragraph separately. Freeze questions before inference; compare candidates in both orders and treat changed winners as inconclusive. Broad or near-even judgments require inspection, not automatic rewriting or score chasing.

Use an existing evaluated content contract for the selected provider. Without a passing independently labeled evaluation, retain review-only status and the manual/rule path. Paid calls need an explicit source scope and budget. Missing evidence is separate from negative evidence. Log model/contract versions and editorial overrides; never credit the model for a human finding. Text judgments do not replace native media playback or source-boundary review. This package makes no model calls and includes no credentials.

## Review and release ownership

Keep thumbnail co-design and edit-feedback loops open. A prepared artifact, model recommendation, earlier selection or absence of comments is not final approval. Bind approval to the exact title/image pair and media version/hash. Material meaning, cut or hook changes reopen affected checks; date-only moves require queue/timing checks, not new semantic inference.

Keep a release table with episode, asset, destination, proposed slot and timezone, parent article, current version, review state, owner/conflict, next action and approval needed. Refresh it when feedback or another publisher changes the plan. Check live queues and directly scheduled posts immediately before approved scheduling. Preserve unrelated posts. If another publisher's calendar is unavailable, disclose the gap. Obtain channel-specific timing preferences; no batch date or frequency becomes an ongoing authorization.

When article-led X distribution is requested, horizontal videos quote their own episode's published article launch post. Keep its public URL; draft/editor links are not valid targets. Verify native quote-plus-video support and the actual attached version. Missing URLs remain dependencies; do not borrow another episode's embed. Adapt LinkedIn copy independently and honor the owner's link-placement preference.

Maintain one episode record linking source ID, approved media version/hash, article draft/public IDs, destination URLs, snippets, captions, timestamps, review evidence and state. Distinguish prepared, review candidate, approved, uploading, processing, scheduled, published and verified. A preview is not a destination readback.

## Delivery and performance

Organize approved team deliveries by recording date, show/content name, approval status, format and asset package. Keep one Content Index for channel placements rather than duplicate media masters. Each package includes editable inputs, current outputs and review evidence. Readback must show the exact destination and files.

Separate user-reported performance from observed analytics. Story plus evidence plus lesson is an editorial hypothesis until measured. Editing approval, source selection approval and publication approval are separate states. Preserve prior versions. Cleanup or deletion requires separate authorization for exact targets.

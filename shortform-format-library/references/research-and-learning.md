# Research, content spikes, and learning

## Start with the supplied spikes

Determine what “spike” means in the provided source: a high-performing post, an audience-retention peak, or a recurring topic/positioning theme. Do not assume these are interchangeable.

- For post analytics, record platform, account, post URL, publication and observation dates, metric and denominator, comparable baseline, sample size, distribution context, and any missing fields.
- For retention graphs, record source video, graph units, exact interval where readable, and what happens there. Rewatching can contribute to a peak; a graph alone does not establish the reason. Inspect the corresponding words and visuals before forming a hypothesis.
- For a topic/theme list, map ideas to the stated themes. It is positioning input, not observed performance.
- For screenshots, transcribe only readable values. If an axis, date, account, or denominator is missing, keep the finding provisional. Ask for the missing source while continuing work that does not depend on it.

Return a comparison table: supplied spike, observed evidence, plausible explanation, topic/hook/format distinction, matching proposed idea, and next test. Explain ranking changes. Do not label an entire format successful when the topic or distribution may explain the result.

## Recurring discovery

Use the user's established source policy. If none exists, propose a bounded weekly pass over the last 30 days, with about 80% of effort on proven audience-relevant reference accounts and 20% on rotating discovery. This is a proposed operating default, not permission to schedule it.

Inventory the accessible sample before picking winners. Compare a post to the same creator's median for similar post type, topic, and age. Prefer comparable views/reach; label likes-only evidence as a proxy and keep metrics separate. Record numerator, baseline, sample size, and window for every multiple. If the baseline is zero or inadequate, leave the multiple unknown. Simple lifetime views divided by age is not a substitute for a matched-age comparison.

Keep posts younger than 48 hours provisional. Discovery can use relevant searches, credited originals in curator posts, and user submissions. Curator engagement is not the original post's performance. Resolve original attribution where possible and deduplicate by platform/post ID.

Inspect the strongest supported candidates in full for structure: opening, delivery, proof timing, visual treatment, pacing, length, CTA, and required assets. Prefer adding a current example to a known format over multiplying near-identical cards.

Suggested editorial gates, configurable to the user's policy:

- Reference: useful structure, evidence saved; performance may be unknown.
- Trial candidate: three relevant creator-relative outliers across two refreshes.
- Supported for this creator: at least five comparable target-account posts with repeated strength on the selected outcome.

These sample gates guide judgment; they do not establish statistical significance. A user-supplied reference may enter immediately without a performance claim.

## Freshness and fatigue

Track publication date and last successful check separately. “Recent” means within the chosen window. “Check due” can mean no successful check for 14 days. Neither means the format works or is stale.

Save dated observations and a short change log. A failed or cached fetch does not advance check dates. Report incomplete coverage and use an ordinary read-only fallback within the same authorized source; do not broaden accounts or acquire quota to bypass a blocker.

At 24 hours and seven days after target publication, record available retention, shares/saves per reach, qualified responses, and attributable leads. Compare similar topics, durations, distribution, and post age. Test an opening, proof order, or visual treatment before retiring a structure. Preserve original evidence and distinguish organic comparisons from controlled experiments.

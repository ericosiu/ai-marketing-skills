# Creative formats and hook treatments

## Stable design, flexible story

Keep typography, palette, caption geometry, safe areas, evidence anchors, and restrained transition behavior consistent. Experiment with the opening promise, proof order, and explanation format. Do not change all three and call the outcome a hook test.

Choose the format the source supports:

| Format | Opening | Middle | Payoff | Required assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence | One claim paired with one readable artifact | Isolate the exact number, excerpt, or decision; explain its meaning | the creator states what the viewer should do | Actual source evidence, provenance, qualifiers |
| Workflow | One concrete outcome or problem | Reveal one step/connection at a time in a stable diagram; pair steps with actual examples | Show the finished process, then return to the creator | Source-supported steps; diagrams labeled as explanations |
| Operator story | A decision, obstacle, or mistake from the recording | Preserve the tension, action, and observed consequence | A useful lesson, then the natural CTA | A real account; no invented stakes, results, or dialogue |

Restraint can be the best treatment. Do not force B-roll into a strong delivery beat. A real product screen can also be poor B-roll if the text cannot be read.

## Opening lab

For one strong source clip, write three short treatment cards before doing expensive full renders:

1. Problem: start with the cost or decision the audience recognizes.
2. Evidence: start with the one visible artifact that supports the promise.
3. Process: start with the useful mechanism the viewer will learn.

Each card specifies the exact spoken words/source timestamps, one on-screen promise, first-frame composition, the full 0–4 second hold, transition into the body, and the hypothesis. Do not create a new spoken claim unless the creator requests a pickup or narration.

Build four-second previews only when the exact media and evidence are available. Watch them at normal speed and 360 px width with sound, then inspect silent readability. Select the clearest preview before producing the full master. If evidence cannot be read alongside the headline and captions, simplify the opening or move proof later.

Keep the body, CTA, palette, and caption system constant for a hook comparison. Preview three, publish one by default. Any live variant test needs its own distribution scope. Do not present non-random organic posts as a controlled experiment.

## Shot-list contract

Each row: output start/end, exact source start/end or asset path, spoken beat, layer/layout, evidence/illustration/brand label, visual job, expected reading demand, and transition. Document missing proof instead of silently generating it.

For numeric claims, preserve what the count measures and when. For ad-count claims, distinguish tested ads from active ads. A number seen halfway through a count-up animation is not evidence of the final count. Don't strengthen either the caption or the cover beyond the verified source.

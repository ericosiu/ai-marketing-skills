# V5 style

A reusable editorial preset. A creator's brief overrides it. The public package contains no private reference footage or review history; the preset does not establish a quality score or predict retention.

## Design

- Export new vertical masters at 1080 × 1920 using the source frame rate. Preserve natural camera color and voice.
- Hold one readable opening headline for four seconds. Avoid chat flashes, evidence montages, and layout changes during the hook.
- Use warm ivory `#F6F5F1`, near-black `#161818`, and warm yellow `#FFE276`. Prefer flat typography and restrained transitions.
- At 1080 px width, begin with a bold headline near 102 px and phrase captions near 94 px. Limit captions to two lines and roughly 880 px width. Rechunk long phrases instead of shrinking text. Verify at 360 px phone width.
- Keep caption width and placement stable within an explanation. Use solid backgrounds or sufficient dark outlines. Do not place yellow text directly over camera footage.
- Put the intro and comment prompt on opaque ivory panels with near-black text. A yellow backing can emphasize the CTA keyword.
- Frame the speaker naturally, with room for gestures. Recalculate crops for each source. Remove duplicate identity labels; preserve spoken credentials and relevant brand identification.

## Evidence and motion

Keep the speaker window, heading hierarchy, caption position, and evidence anchor stable through each explanation. Reveal information when the spoken idea changes. Hold each state long enough to read alongside captions.

Use real screenshots for evidence, labeled diagrams for explanation, and footage for story beats. Crop to a complete, legible excerpt. Logos should land with the spoken brand name. Never replace available evidence with a generated interface or present an illustration as a measured result.

Return to the speaker for the payoff and the recording's CTA. End with a brief readable hold. Avoid unrelated stock, glossy motion cards, and constant word animation.

## Timing and review

Anchor picture and audio to the same source frames. Preserve natural word handles, combine PCM segments, and encode AAC once. Source fps and crop coordinates are recording-specific.

Review the final file continuously with sound, then inspect opening, proof, transitions, and ending at phone size. Sampled stills, successful decoding, and audio correlation cannot establish editorial quality or lip sync alone.

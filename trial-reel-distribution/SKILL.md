# Trial Reel Distribution

Trial reels are shown to non-followers. That makes them a distribution channel for content that already works, not a lab for content that has never been tested. This skill runs trials that way: proven feed winners, reposted unchanged, in front of a new audience.

## Principle

- A trial slot is reach in front of people who do not follow you. Spending it on an unproven video wastes the slot: most unproven content fails in the feed too, so the trial only re-confirms the failure to a colder audience.
- Variant testing answers the wrong question. Five rounds of edit variants on unproven masters typically move the best result from low to slightly less low, because the bottleneck is the idea, not the edit. Distribution skips the question: the feed already ran the test, and reach is the result.
- The video is the proven part. Change anything about it and you are testing again. The only new elements are the caption and the export.
- Winners compound. A trial that travels gets graduated to the feed, and every new feed post that proves itself becomes fresh stock for the pool.

## When to use

- You post reels to a feed regularly and have at least a few months of posts with reach data.
- Your trial reels underperform and you have been using them to test edits, hooks, or unproven clips.
- You want a repeatable non-follower reach lane that does not require producing new content.

## Inputs you need

1. Your feed posts from the last 90 days, ranked by reach (your analytics tool's per-post reach export is enough).
2. The original video files for your top posts, or a way to download the finished posts back.
3. A scheduler/analytics tool that can schedule trial reels and report per-post reach (for example Metricool, or the native app plus manual tracking). Trials do not count against your feed posting cadence.
4. A graduation bar. 1,000 reach per trial is the worked setting for a mid-size account; scale it so only genuinely proven posts clear it on your account.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Build the pool

- Rank your feed posts by reach over the last 90 days. Take the top 30 or so. These are proven: each one already earned its reach once.
- Exclude anything you have already run as a distribution trial.
- Record each pool item's original reach. You will compare the trial result against it later.

### Step 2: Prepare each post

- Keep the video content exactly as posted. No speed changes, no music swaps, no new hook, no re-edit.
- Re-export the file so the upload is a fresh file, not a byte-identical duplicate of the original post. Same content, new export. Byte-identical re-uploads trip duplicate detection on both the platform and most schedulers.
- Write a fresh caption: new wording, same topic, and the same call-to-action rules you use on the feed. Do not copy the original caption verbatim.

### Step 3: Schedule in batches

- Post about 5 trials per day, spread across the day rather than in one burst.
- Work down the pool in reach order, best first. The strongest proven posts deserve the first slots.

### Step 4: Read the results

- Read reach per post at 48 to 72 hours. Reach is the metric; likes and comments are secondary.
- A post whose analytics row is still empty at read time is pending, not zero. Recheck it on the next pass before judging it.
- Log each trial's reach next to its original feed reach.

### Step 5: Graduate the winners

- Any trial at or over your graduation bar (1,000 reach in the worked example) is a winner.
- Post the winner to your main feed, unchanged, and put it into your repost lifecycle: spaced repeat touches over the following months instead of a one-off.
- Respect your feed cadence cap (for example 2 feed posts per day). If graduation day is full, the winner posts on the first open day. Queue discipline beats same-day posting.

### Step 6: Apply the winner rule

- When a trial breaks the bar, pause the batch flow and re-optimize around that winner first: it is the template for the next level (for example, study what its topic and format share with your other over-bar posts).
- Then raise the bar and resume. The worked ladder is 1,000 reach first, then 10,000.
- Do not let batch timing decide this silently. The pause is a decision point, not a scheduling accident.

### Step 7: Refill the pool

- Every new feed post that proves itself (reach clearly above your recent median) joins the pool.
- The lane feeds itself: feed winners become trials, trial winners return to the feed with a lifecycle, and new originals keep entering at the top.

## Worked example (anonymized)

One account switched its trial program from variant testing to distribution:

- Before: 5 rounds of variant testing on unproven videos. Best single variant across all rounds: 366 reach.
- After: the top feed posts of the last 90 days reposted as trials, video unchanged, fresh caption and export.
- First 8 distribution posts: 5 of 8 cleared 1,000 reach. Top post: 6,107 reach. Total: about 16,900 reach.
- Per post, distribution delivered roughly 11x the reach of the variant-testing approach.
- The first bar-breaker triggered the winner rule: batches paused, the winner graduated to the feed, and the next bar was set at 10,000 reach.
- Distribution then restarted as a standing lane with the rest of the pool, not as another one-off test.

## Pitfalls

- Testing unproven content in trials. The most common failure mode. If it has not proven itself in the feed, the trial slot will not save it.
- Editing the video on the repost. The video is the proven asset. A new hook, a new cut, or a music swap turns distribution back into testing.
- Uploading the byte-identical file. Re-export first. Duplicate guards reject identical files, and identical posts underperform.
- Judging on a pending analytics row. Empty means not synced yet. Wait one more cycle.
- Flooding the feed with graduates. Winners queue behind your daily feed cap. A winner posted on the first open day loses nothing.
- Optimizing the edit when the idea is the bottleneck. If your variants cluster at low reach, stop variant testing and check whether the underlying posts ever proved themselves anywhere.

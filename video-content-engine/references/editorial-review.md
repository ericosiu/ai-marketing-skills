# Editorial review

Use this contract for recording-based long-form, mid-form, and vertical edits. Apply the user's current brief and approved brand profile first. These rules describe editing decisions; they do not certify any existing batch.

## Before selecting cuts

Inventory each original camera, screen share, slide deck, presentation feed, microphone, and computer playback-audio track. Record source ID, hash, duration, frame rate, dimensions, offset, and accessible rights. Synchronize on visible and audible events. Check drift near both ends. Do not assume the composite contains every track or that separate tracks start together.

Build a visual-proof map: spoken reference, source time, required screen or object, output placement, readable crop, confidentiality treatment, and missing dependency. When the speaker says “look at this,” the viewer should see the actual subject. Prefer the screen as the main image with a speaker inset when the demonstration carries the argument. Preserve cursor and relevant interface context. Label reconstructions and do not invent missing results.

## Select a complete portfolio

List all plausible long-form, mid-form, vertical, and promotional candidates before selecting a release wave. For each, record source spans, opening line, tension or story turn, evidence, payoff, format, runtime estimate, job, dependencies, and selection reason. Include deferred and rejected candidates so “best moments” means a reviewed inventory, not the first excerpts found.

Do not impose a twelve-asset quota or turn every chapter into a clip. Repeated promises compete with one another. A strong vertical candidate can be a complete story, objection, demonstration, mistake, or framework; high energy alone is insufficient. Separate self-contained editorial assets from teasers.

## Design the opening before rendering

Write the first thirty seconds as beats: exact spoken line, source span, visible evidence, viewer promise, and transition into the body. Lead with a concrete result, problem, stakes, disagreement, or surprising observation supported by the source. Generic advice, production handoffs, and decorative headlines do not establish a hook.

Show the real artifact early in demonstrations. Preserve qualifiers when moving a result into the opening. Remove duplicated cold-open material from the body only when the new transition stays coherent. Hook variants are separate review options, not substitutes for the complete episode.

For each vertical asset, record its exact opening, context needed by a new viewer, payoff, and final line. Avoid an ending that merely stops because the requested duration was reached.

## Build and review the edit

Resolve cut points against exact-source word or sentence timestamps and run the boundary audit. Keep natural audio handles where they protect cadence. A boundary audit cannot establish that reordered ideas form a coherent argument.

Create deliberate layouts for each orientation. Preserve source frame rate unless a justified delivery conversion is needed. Keep screens legible; avoid stretching or cropping faces unnaturally. Use exact synchronized computer audio for demonstrations when available, and check for duplicate playback or echo.

Review the full rendered asset with audio using an authorized tool or reviewer. Inspect every join, layout transition, screen reference, caption, qualifier, and ending. Sample frames, ASR, decode checks, and loudness measurements support this review but cannot replace it. If full review is unavailable, record the gate as pending and describe the artifact as a technical draft.

Each review record identifies:

- asset ID, render version, hash, reviewer, and review date;
- actual opening, promised outcome, proof coverage, and ending verdict;
- complete-playback coverage and specific defect timecodes;
- technical, editorial, caption, claims, rights, and packaging gate results;
- requested repairs, owner, next version, and approval state.

Missing screen evidence or a weak opening blocks editorial readiness. Preserve the failed version and its feedback, repair it, then review the changed sequence and complete revised output.

## Save learning without leaking projects

Store brand choices separately from general editing rules. Keep private source links, names, exact transcript excerpts, comments, and performance figures in the authorized project store. Public skill updates should describe the transferable failure and correction using generic or fictional examples. Direct corrections may update the workflow immediately; performance claims need comparable measured results.
